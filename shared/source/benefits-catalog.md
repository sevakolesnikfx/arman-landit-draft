# Landed: the full benefits catalog

Every scheme researched for the Landed demo, in one flat list. 182 entries across two sweeps on 5 August 2026: the base catalog (1-118) plus student finance, England city-region, Scotland and Wales extras, Northern Ireland, and the week-one practical layer (119-182). All rates 2026/27 (April 2026) unless noted. Companion files: Landed_benefits_research_2026-08-05.md (status matrix, gateway tests, engine notes, demo corrections) and landed_benefits_data_2026-08-05.json (engine-ready seed).

How to read an entry: "public funds: yes" means the scheme is on the paragraph 6.2 Immigration Rules list and is blocked for anyone with the NRPF visa condition. "NRPF" states whether an NRPF holder can get it in practice, and why. "who" uses the app's status groups: Worker (Skilled Worker and Health and Care), Student, Graduate, Family visa, BN(O), EUSS settled, EUSS pre-settled, Refugee (includes humanitarian protection), Ukraine (all Ukraine schemes), ILR, Asylum seeker. "everyone" means no status test at all.

Two gateway tests apply on top of everything: the Habitual Residence Test (means-tested claims by people with recourse: right to reside plus roughly 1 to 3 months of factual residence; refugees, Ukraine and Afghan arrivals exempt) and the past presence test for disability benefits (104 of the last 156 weeks in GB; same exemptions).

Disclaimer for any surface this feeds: "Landed provides information only, not legal or financial advice. Eligibility varies by individual circumstance and immigration status. Always verify with official sources or a qualified adviser before applying."

## A. Legal-status actions that unlock money

### 1. Change of Conditions (lift NRPF)
Free Home Office application to remove the NRPF condition from current leave. If granted, the whole public-funds tier opens until leave expires.
- worth: unlocks UC, Child Benefit and everything in section Q
- public funds: no · NRPF: yes, it exists only for NRPF holders
- who: Family visa, private life route, BN(O) (work and study routes only in particularly compelling circumstances)
- get it: destitute, or at risk of destitution within 3 months, or compelling child-welfare reasons, or exceptional financial circumstances; full disclosure of every bank account
- apply: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/application-for-change-of-conditions-of-leave-to-allow-access-to-public-funds-if-your-circumstances-change
- stat: Q1 2026: 72% acceptance, 34 days average (Home Office reported); 57% and up to 93 days on the Unity Project's adjusted numbers; 64% historic grant rate (Migration Observatory)
- strengthen: Tier 1 priority (72 working hours) if street homeless, Tier 2 (14 working days) for other serious vulnerability; explain every large transaction; 5-year family route holders should get advice first (risk of being moved to the 10-year route)
- source: https://unity-project.org.uk/analysis-of-home-office-change-of-conditions-data-q1-2026/

### 2. Visa fee waiver (in-country extensions)
Waives the £1,321 application fee and the Immigration Health Surcharge for people who cannot afford them. Destitution not required, inability to afford is enough.
- worth: about £3,900 per adult per 30-month extension cycle
- public funds: no · NRPF: yes
- who: Family visa (5 and 10 year routes), private life, discretionary leave, BN(O), trafficking victims
- get it: cannot afford fee plus IHS after essential living needs; 6 months of statements for every account
- apply: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/applications-for-a-fee-waiver-and-refunds/fee-waiver-human-rights-based-and-other-specified-applications
- stat: processing averaged 69 to 168 days across 2023-24; backlog peaked at 38,426 (ICIBI, March 2025)
- strengthen: explain every transaction over £250; submitting before leave expires extends s3C protection
- source: ICIBI fee waiver inspection, March 2025

### 3. National Insurance number
Free, online only. Makes work clean, builds contributory entitlement, and is required for childcare accounts, student finance and LISAs.
- worth: enabler for sections E, F and O
- public funds: no · NRPF: yes
- who: everyone with the right to work
- get it: live in the UK, right to work; up to 4 weeks; you can start work first using the eVisa share code
- apply: https://www.gov.uk/apply-national-insurance-number (never pay a third-party site; some BRPs already show a NINo)

## B. Health

### 4. GP registration
Free primary care and the gateway to all NHS services. No ID, no proof of address, no immigration status required (NHS policy). Homeless patients can use the surgery's address.
- public funds: no · NRPF: yes · who: everyone, including no status
- apply: https://www.nhs.uk/nhs-services/gps/how-to-register-with-a-gp-surgery/
- strengthen: if refused, ask for the refusal in writing

### 5. NHS services that are always free
A&E and urgent treatment (until admitted), all GP care, contraception and sexual health clinics (walk-in), treatment of specified infectious diseases (TB, HIV, Covid), treatment of conditions caused by torture, FGM, domestic or sexual violence, NHS 111.
- public funds: no · NRPF: yes · who: everyone · region: England (parallel exemptions in the other nations)
- source: https://www.gov.uk/guidance/nhs-entitlements-migrant-health-guide

### 6. Immigration Health Surcharge entitlement
Visa holders who paid the IHS (£1,035/yr, students £776/yr) get full NHS care like a resident: hospital treatment, maternity, mental health. Health and Care Worker visa holders are exempt from paying with the same access.
- public funds: no · NRPF: yes · who: Worker, Student, Graduate, Family visa, BN(O)
- strengthen: if a hospital wrongly invoices, show visa validity and ask the Overseas Visitor Manager to cancel
- source: https://www.gov.uk/healthcare-immigration-application

### 7. NHS Low Income Scheme (HC2/HC3)
Means-tested help with health costs, no immigration condition, no benefits link. HC2 = free prescriptions, free NHS dental, free sight tests, glasses vouchers, travel to treatment. Asylum seekers on s95 get HC2 automatically.
- worth: routinely £100 to £400+ a year for anyone on regular medication or needing dental work
- public funds: no · NRPF: yes · who: everyone · region: England and Wales (equivalents elsewhere)
- get it: savings under £16,000; income at or below assessed requirements (students count as low income in term time); form HC1
- apply: https://apply-for-help-with-nhs-costs.nhsbsa.nhs.uk (about 18 working days)
- source: https://www.nhsbsa.nhs.uk/nhs-low-income-scheme

### 8. Prescription charges and the prepayment certificate
England 2026/27: £9.90 per item, frozen. PPC: £32.05 for 3 months, £114.50 for 12: pays for itself at 12+ items a year. Scotland, Wales and NI: free for everyone.
- worth: £100+/yr saved with a 12-month PPC on 2 items a month
- public funds: no · NRPF: yes · who: everyone
- apply: https://www.nhsbsa.nhs.uk/help-nhs-prescription-costs/prescription-prepayment-certificates-ppcs

### 9. Maternity exemption certificate (matex)
Free prescriptions and free NHS dental while pregnant and for 12 months after birth. Issued on pregnancy alone, no status condition.
- public funds: no · NRPF: yes · who: everyone pregnant
- apply: ask the midwife at the booking appointment, they submit it
- source: https://www.nhsbsa.nhs.uk/help-nhs-prescription-costs/maternity-exemption-certificates

### 10. NHS dental (banded charges)
England from April 2026: Band 1 £27.90, Band 2 £76.60, Band 3 £332.10. Free: under-18s, pregnancy plus 12 months, HC2 holders. Scotland: free exams for all, free care under 26, adults pay 80% capped £384. Wales: package system capped £384.
- public funds: no · NRPF: yes · who: everyone
- strengthen: ask to be seen "on the NHS"; check HC2 before Band 3 work
- source: https://www.nhs.uk/nhs-services/dentists/dental-costs/

### 11. Free vitamins
Healthy Start vitamins free with the scheme (England/Wales/NI, including the NRPF extension). Scotland: free pregnancy vitamins for every pregnant woman, no means test, just ask the midwife.
- public funds: no · NRPF: yes
- source: https://www.gov.scot/policies/maternal-and-child-health/free-vitamins-for-all-pregnant-women/

## C. Education and school costs

### 12. Free school meals, England (NRPF route + September 2026 expansion)
From September 2026 every child in a UC household qualifies (earnings cap abolished). Permanent NRPF route: income caps £22,700 (1 child) / £26,300 (2+) outside London, £31,200 / £34,800 in London, savings under £16,000. Infants universal. Registration unlocks pupil premium and FSM-linked help.
- worth: about £495 per child per year (DfE)
- public funds: no · NRPF: yes, dedicated route · who: everyone with school-age children
- apply: https://www.gov.uk/apply-free-school-meals
- stat: 500,000+ more pupils eligible from September 2026 (DfE)
- note: in London every state primary child already eats free regardless of status (entry 131)
- source: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/free-school-meals-guidance-for-schools-and-local-authorities/providing-free-school-meals-to-families-with-no-recourse-to-public-funds-nrpf

### 13. Free breakfast clubs, England primary
30+ minutes free before-school childcare with breakfast at participating schools (about 2,750 by September 2026). No application, no means test, no immigration condition.
- worth: up to £450/yr plus about 95 hours childcare per child (government figures)
- public funds: no · NRPF: yes · who: every child at a participating school
- stat: 10 million breakfasts served by June 2026 (GOV.UK)
- source: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/free-breakfast-clubs-guidance-for-schools-and-trusts

### 14. 16 to 19 Bursary Fund, England
College money: vulnerable groups bursary up to £1,200/yr, plus discretionary help with travel, equipment and meals set by the institution. Lawfully resident students under DfE residency rules get discretionary support; accompanied asylum-seeker students get in-kind help only.
- public funds: no · NRPF: depends on residency category · who: dependant children of most routes, EUSS, Refugee, Ukraine, BN(O), ILR
- apply: through the school or college. https://www.gov.uk/1619-bursary-fund

### 15. Free ESOL courses, England
Free English classes: fully funded if unemployed or earning under £26,800/yr (2026/27 adult skills threshold). Refugees, Ukraine, BN(O) (day one), Family and EUSS generally qualify; sponsored Skilled Worker and Student visas are excluded from adult skills funding (charity and council classes stay open to them).
- public funds: no · NRPF: depends on route
- apply: local FE college, https://nationalcareers.service.gov.uk/find-a-course
- source: adult skills fund rules 2026/27, GOV.UK

### 16. Public libraries
Free membership, computers, wifi, cheap printing, children's activities, often free e-books and language apps. No immigration condition.
- public funds: no · NRPF: yes · who: everyone
- apply: https://www.gov.uk/local-library-services

### 17. Free home to school transport
Distance-based right with no immigration test: free transport beyond 2 miles (under 8) or 3 miles (8 to 16) to the nearest suitable school. England low-income "extended rights" (2 to 6 miles, ages 11-16) hang off FSM, which NRPF families can hold via the NRPF route.
- public funds: no · NRPF: yes · who: everyone · region: GB (distances vary by nation)
- apply: council transport team, https://www.gov.uk/free-school-transport

### 18. School uniform grants, England councils
No national scheme; a minority of councils pay roughly £30 to £200 per child, usually FSM-linked (the FSM NRPF route can unlock it). School hardship funds and PTA uniform banks have no status test. Example: Bexley £37.50 to £62.50, exceptional circumstances only.
- public funds: no · NRPF: depends on council criteria · region: England, per council
- apply: council website via https://www.gov.uk/find-local-council

### 19. School Essentials Grant, Wales
£125 per child per year, £200 for year 7 entrants, for uniform, kit and equipment. Explicitly open to NRPF families and asylum-seeking families (NASS number accepted).
- public funds: no · NRPF: yes (unusual and worth surfacing) · region: Wales
- apply: through the county council, https://www.gov.wales/school-essentials-grant

### 20. School clothing grant, Scotland
Statutory annual grant: minimum £120 primary / £150 secondary. Gateway is the FSM benefit list; asylum support counts as a qualifying category (ARC card), pure NRPF without asylum support usually cannot qualify.
- public funds: no · NRPF: depends · region: Scotland
- apply: council, usually the combined FSM/clothing form (opens May/June)

### 21. EMA Wales
£40 per week during term for 16 to 18 year olds staying in education, household income £24,570 or less (one child) / £27,273 (more). Standard categories need settled status plus 3 years residence; refugees qualify and others are case by case with extra evidence.
- public funds: no · NRPF: depends · region: Wales (abolished in England)
- get it: eligible full-time course; 100% attendance rule
- apply: https://www.studentfinancewales.co.uk/further-education-funding/education-maintenance-allowance/

### 22. Free school meals, Scotland
Universal P1 to P5, no income or status test. P6+ via benefits including Scottish Child Payment or UC (earned income £995/month or less); families on asylum support qualify.
- public funds: no · NRPF: yes for P1-P5, depends after · region: Scotland
- apply: council, https://www.mygov.scot/school-meals

### 23. Universal primary free school meals, Wales
Every primary pupil, rollout complete, no status test (NRPF Network confirms). Secondary expansion to UC households starts September 2026 (years 7 and 8 first); councils can grant discretionary FSM to NRPF children and Welsh Government encourages it.
- public funds: no · NRPF: yes (primary) · region: Wales
- apply: automatic for primary

### 24. University hardship funds
Every UK university runs one; most now run a separate international student fund. Assessed on hardship evidence, not benefits: the flagship NRPF-safe cash for students. Sector awards typically £500 to £3,000 (Save the Student); Greenwich international fund up to £1,000/yr.
- public funds: no · NRPF: yes · who: Student, Graduate (while enrolled), any enrolled student
- get it: enrolled; evidenced unexpected shortfall; some funds require under £500 across all accounts
- apply: search "[university] hardship fund"
- strengthen: ask whether the award is a gift or repayable before accepting

### 25. King's Trust Development Awards (16 to 30)
Cash awards removing cost barriers to education, training or work: course fees to Level 3, tools, equipment, licences, transport to a new job. Criteria are age, UK residence and low work hours; no benefit or settlement requirement published. Amounts unfixed (third-party listings cite about £160 typical).
- public funds: no · NRPF: yes (confirm case by case) · who: 16-30s studying under 14h/week, unemployed or working under 16h/week
- apply: https://www.kingstrust.org.uk/how-we-can-help/free-funding-to-train-or-start-work

### 26. Student discount platforms
TOTUM (free digital tier, TOTUM+ £14.99/yr), UNiDAYS, Student Beans: verification is enrolment, not status. International students qualify.
- public funds: no · NRPF: yes · who: Student, Graduate while enrolled

## D. Childcare

### 27. 15 hours universal early education (3 and 4 year olds), England
Every 3 and 4 year old, 570 hours a year from the term after the third birthday. No work test, no income test, no immigration test.
- public funds: no · NRPF: yes, explicitly · who: everyone
- apply: directly through any participating nursery or childminder
- source: https://www.gov.uk/help-with-childcare-costs/free-childcare-and-education-for-2-to-4-year-olds

### 28. 15 hours for disadvantaged 2 year olds (NRPF route), England
Dedicated DfE route for NRPF families: income caps £26,500 (1 child) / £30,600 (2+) outside London, £34,500 / £38,600 in London, savings under £16,000. Children of s95/s98/s4 families qualify.
- public funds: no · NRPF: yes, dedicated route · who: Worker, Student, Graduate, Family visa, BN(O), Asylum seeker, no status
- apply: council early years team (government NRPF form)
- strengthen: apply the term before the place is needed
- source: GOV.UK early years entitlements for NRPF families

### 29. 30 hours working-parent childcare (from 9 months), England
Up to £7,500 per child per year (DfE). The partner rule: an NRPF parent cannot apply alone, but a partner with recourse applies and includes them; both must meet the work test (£2,643.68 per quarter each, neither over £100k). Single NRPF parents and all-NRPF couples are excluded. Reconfirm every 3 months.
- public funds: no · NRPF: depends, partner rule · who: applicant needs recourse (EUSS, Refugee, Ukraine, ILR, British/Irish partner)
- apply: https://www.gov.uk/apply-30-hours-free-childcare
- source: https://www.nrpfnetwork.org.uk/information-and-resources/rights-and-entitlements/services-for-children-and-families/early-education-and-childcare/working-parents

### 30. Tax-Free Childcare
£2 added per £8 paid in: up to £2,000 per child per year (£4,000 disabled). Not a public fund. Same partner rule as the 30 hours. Cannot combine with the UC childcare element.
- public funds: no · NRPF: depends, partner rule · region: UK
- apply: same childcare account, https://www.gov.uk/apply-for-tax-free-childcare

## E. Work and tax (all NRPF-safe)

### 31. Statutory Sick Pay
£123.25/week (2026/27) or 80% of average earnings if lower, up to 28 weeks. Since April 2026: paid from day one, no earnings floor (about 1.3m low-paid workers newly covered, DWP estimate).
- public funds: no · NRPF: yes · who: any employee with right to work
- strengthen: if refused, demand form SSP1; the Fair Work Agency enforces
- apply: automatic via payroll, https://www.gov.uk/statutory-sick-pay

### 32. Statutory Maternity and Paternity Pay
SMP: 90% of earnings for 6 weeks then £194.32/week for 33 weeks (2026/27). Paternity: 2 weeks at £194.32. Needs 26 weeks with the same employer by the 15th week before the due date, earnings £129+/week.
- public funds: no · NRPF: yes
- strengthen: if not qualifying, the employer must issue SMP1, which supports Maternity Allowance
- apply: https://www.gov.uk/maternity-pay-leave

### 33. Maternity Allowance
£194.32/week for up to 39 weeks (up to about £7,600) for women who cannot get SMP: recent job changers, self-employed, agency. Confirmed NOT a public fund (Home Office v21.0). The single largest NRPF-safe cash sum in this catalog.
- public funds: no · NRPF: yes · who: any working status
- get it: employed or self-employed 26 of the 66 weeks before the due date (any mix of jobs); £30+/week in any 13 of them
- apply: form MA1, https://www.gov.uk/maternity-allowance/how-to-claim
- strengthen: weeks need not be consecutive or with one employer

### 34. National Minimum Wage enforcement
April 2026: £12.71/hr at 21+, £10.85 at 18-20, £8.00 at 16-17 and apprentices. HMRC recovers up to 6 years of underpayment, free, can be anonymous. Common in care, hospitality, delivery, cleaning.
- public funds: no · NRPF: yes
- apply: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/pay-and-work-rights-complaints

### 35. Holiday pay (5.6 weeks)
28 days paid leave full-time, accrues from day one, includes agency and zero-hours. Untaken statutory leave must be paid out when a job ends.
- public funds: no · NRPF: yes
- strengthen: check the final payslip for accrued holiday on leaving
- source: https://www.gov.uk/holiday-entitlement-rights

### 36. Workplace pension auto-enrolment
Employer pays minimum 3% of qualifying earnings (£6,240 to £50,270 band) for workers 22+ earning £10,000+: about £713/yr free on a £30,000 salary. Opting out forfeits it; leaving the UK does not.
- public funds: no · NRPF: yes
- source: https://www.gov.uk/workplace-pensions

### 37. HMRC tax refunds
Emergency-tax overpayments back via P800 (automatic), P50 (stopped work mid-year), P85 (leaving the UK); 4 tax years back.
- public funds: no · NRPF: yes
- strengthen: check the first payslip; 1257L W1/M1 or BR usually means overpaying
- apply: https://www.gov.uk/claim-tax-refund

### 38. Uniform and work expenses tax relief
Flat rate £60/yr standard (£12 to a basic-rate payer), healthcare £125, ambulance £185, some trades £120 to £140. Backdatable 4 years, no receipts needed.
- public funds: no · NRPF: yes
- apply: https://www.gov.uk/tax-relief-for-employees/uniforms-work-clothing-and-tools

### 39. Marriage Allowance
Non-earning spouse transfers £1,260 of allowance: up to £252/yr, backdatable 4 years (about £1,000 first claim). Very relevant to non-working dependant partners. Needs NI numbers, no immigration condition.
- public funds: no · NRPF: yes
- apply: free at https://www.gov.uk/apply-marriage-allowance (the lower earner claims)

## F. Contributory benefits (year 2 to 3 in the UK)

### 40. New Style Jobseeker's Allowance
£95.55/week at 25+ (2026/27) for up to 182 days. Needs Class 1 NI in the last 2 complete tax years, so realistic from year 2-3. Savings and partner income ignored. Claiming never breaches NRPF.
- public funds: no · NRPF: yes · who: any status with the NI record (full-time students no)
- apply: https://www.gov.uk/guidance/new-style-jobseekers-allowance
- note: losing sponsored work also has visa consequences, show both

### 41. New Style ESA
Same contribution logic for people too ill to work: £95.55/week assessment rate (25+); support group adds £50.35/week, no time limit. Self-employed Class 2 counts (unlike JSA).
- public funds: no · NRPF: yes
- strengthen: claim the day SSP ends so the periods link
- apply: https://www.gov.uk/guidance/new-style-employment-and-support-allowance

## G. Family safety net

### 42. Section 17 Children Act support
Councils must help any "child in need" regardless of the family's immigration status: accommodation and subsistence for destitute families. The designed floor for NRPF families; cannot be refused just because of NRPF.
- public funds: no · NRPF: yes, by design · who: any family with a child under 18 facing destitution · region: England and Wales (Scottish and NI equivalents)
- apply: council children's services front door; out of hours the emergency duty team
- strengthen: request in writing; ask for interim support during assessment; Project 17 and Together with Migrant Children challenge unlawful refusals
- source: https://togethermigrantchildren.org.uk/accessing-section-17-support/

### 43. Healthy Start (England, Wales, NI) with the NRPF extension
Prepaid food card: £4.65/week pregnancy and ages 1-4, £9.30/week per baby under 1 (April 2026 rates), plus free vitamins. Standard route needs UC with earnings £408/month or less. NRPF extension: at least one BRITISH child under 4 and earned income £408/month or less; does not cover pregnant women or non-British children (as of August 2026).
- public funds: no · NRPF: depends, British-child route · region: England, Wales, NI
- apply: https://www.healthystart.nhs.uk/how-to-apply/ (NRPF route: healthystartnrpf@nhsbsa.nhs.uk, decision in 5 working days)

### 44. Best Start Foods, Scotland
Scotland's food card, deliberately opened to NRPF: £5.60/week pregnancy and ages 1-3, £11.20/week per baby under 1. NRPF route: child under 3, Scottish address, income £1,878.22/month or less single (£2,223.24 couple). Asylum seekers explicitly included.
- public funds: no · NRPF: yes, dedicated route · region: Scotland
- apply: https://www.mygov.scot/best-start-grant-best-start-foods (phone: say "I have no access to public funds")

### 45. Sure Start Maternity Grant
£500 one-off for a first baby, on a qualifying benefit. Claim window: 11 weeks before the due date to 6 months after birth.
- public funds: yes · NRPF: no · who: Refugee, Ukraine, EUSS settled, ILR, qualifying pre-settled · region: England, Wales, NI
- strengthen: new refugees with a baby under 6 months can still claim once UC starts
- apply: form SF100, https://www.gov.uk/sure-start-maternity-grant

### 46. Best Start Grant, Scotland
Three one-off payments, every child not just the first: Pregnancy and Baby £796.65 first child / £398.35 others; Early Learning £331.95; School Age £331.95.
- public funds: yes · NRPF: no · who: recourse groups on qualifying benefits (under-18 parents need no benefit) · region: Scotland
- apply: one form with Scottish Child Payment, https://www.mygov.scot/best-start-grant-best-start-foods

## H. Council and crisis support

### 47. Crisis and Resilience Fund, England (replaced the Household Support Fund, April 2026)
Council crisis help to March 2029 (£858m for 2026-27): vouchers, energy top-ups, food, white goods. The standard route runs on s1 Localism Act 2011, a listed public fund: NRPF households are NOT eligible (guidance para 28). Councils can still help NRPF families under s17 Children Act, Care Act or public health powers (paras 29-30).
- public funds: yes (standard route) · NRPF: no standard, depends via social services routes (get written confirmation of the legal power used)
- who: Refugee, Ukraine, EUSS, ILR via standard route
- stat: predecessor schemes: 60% of applications awarded, average £214 (End Furniture Poverty 2024/25)
- apply: upper-tier council via https://www.gov.uk/find-local-council

### 48. Local welfare assistance, England
Councils' own crisis schemes (now mostly CRF-funded): white goods, furniture, food vouchers, fuel. Same public-funds analysis as the CRF. 48 councils (31% of England's population) ran no scheme in 2024/25. Some councils run separate migrant destitution funds (example: Hackney).
- public funds: yes (standard route) · NRPF: no standard, depends locally
- finder: https://endfurniturepoverty.org/research-campaigns/rebuilding-crisis-support-local-welfare-assistance/local-welfare-assistance-finder/

### 49. Scottish Welfare Fund
Crisis Grants (average £130) and Community Care Grants (average £720). Listed public fund; statutory guidance says no award should be made to a person with NRPF. About 65% of crisis applications awarded in 2025-26.
- public funds: yes · NRPF: no · who: recourse groups in Scotland · region: Scotland
- apply: https://www.mygov.scot/scottish-welfare-fund

### 50. Discretionary Assistance Fund, Wales
Emergency Assistance Payments (food, gas, electricity, emergency travel): NOT a public fund, and Welsh guidance explicitly includes NRPF people. Up to 3 in 12 months, 7 days apart, decisions usually within 1 working day. The white-goods IAP arm is benefit-gated.
- public funds: no (EAP) · NRPF: yes (EAP), no (IAP) · who: anyone in Wales in extreme hardship, including Asylum seekers · region: Wales
- apply: https://www.gov.wales/discretionary-assistance-fund-daf or 0800 859 5924

### 51. Council tax discounts and exemptions
Composition-based, not means-tested, so NRPF-safe: full-time student exemption (Class N, all-student household pays nothing, and it covers the student's non-working non-British spouse or dependant), 25% single person discount, 50% all-disregarded. Never confuse with Council Tax Reduction (entry 66), which is a public fund.
- worth: 100% off for student households (£1,500 to £2,500+ by band); 25% living alone
- public funds: no · NRPF: yes · region: England, Scotland, Wales
- apply: https://www.gov.uk/apply-for-council-tax-discount
- strengthen: ask for backdating to course or tenancy start

### 52. Discretionary Housing Payments (and England's CRF Housing Payments)
Rent shortfall top-ups. Gated on Housing Benefit or the UC housing element, so never available under NRPF anywhere in GB. England folded DHPs into CRF Housing Payments in April 2026 (guidance para 31 says explicitly: not payable to NRPF).
- public funds: gateway benefits are · NRPF: no · who: recourse groups with a rent shortfall
- apply: council (England CRF form; Scotland and Wales DHP forms)

### 53. Homelessness help under NRPF
Part 7 homelessness assistance and social housing are public funds: closed. Open regardless of status: free council advice (s179, owed to everyone), s17 accommodation for families, Care Act accommodation for adults with care needs, severe weather (SWEP) beds, day centres, StreetLink outreach, Shelter helpline 0808 800 4444.
- public funds: Part 7 yes, social services routes no · NRPF: advice and social services routes yes
- note: Ukraine scheme holders have FULL homelessness rights, never treat them as NRPF
- apply: council housing options; https://thestreetlink.org.uk/; https://homeless.org.uk/

### 54. Rent deposit and bond schemes
Council schemes usually sit inside homelessness prevention or CRF Housing Payments (closed to NRPF). Charity-run bond schemes have no immigration test, postcode-dependent. Families with children can get deposits and rent in advance under s17.
- public funds: depends on funding route · NRPF: depends

The master gate for university money in England, 2026/27. Tuition fee loan up to £9,790 (verified); maintenance loan up to £9,118 living with parents, £10,830 away from home, £14,135 in London. Student finance is NOT public funds: NRPF does not block it, the category list does.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Refugee (+ family) | home | yes | yes | immediate, no 3-year wait; mid-course upgrade from the next academic year |
| Humanitarian protection (+ family) | home | yes | yes | immediate for courses starting from Aug 2023 (England; check per nation) |
| Ukraine schemes | home | yes | yes | immediate, confirmed in the 2026/27 rules |
| Afghan schemes | home | yes | yes | immediate |
| Stateless, Calais leave, s67 | home | yes | yes | immediate from grant |
| EUSS settled | home | yes | yes | 3 years ordinary residence (EEA residence can count) |

- public funds: no (own residence rules instead) · region: England rules shown, all four nations run parallel category lists
- source: UKCISA categories, GOV.UK how-assessed-and-paid 2026/27 (verified), Commons Library CBP-10708

### 120. Sanctuary scholarships (asylum seekers and NRPF students)
University-funded fee waivers plus living stipends for people locked out of student finance. 80+ universities offer one (STAR). 2026 examples: Sheffield full fees + £10,300/yr (5 awards, deadline 29 June 2026), Cardiff 12 awards fees + £4,000 to £12,590/yr, Bristol full fees + full stipend. Small numbers, apply direct to the university, deadlines cluster Feb to July.
- public funds: no · NRPF: yes, the target group · who: Asylum seekers, refused with fresh claims, some limited-leave holders (most schemes exclude anyone who already qualifies for student finance)
- directory: https://star-network.org.uk/access-to-university/scholarships/list/ and https://www.reuk.org/university-and-higher-education

### 121. NHS Learning Support Fund
Non-repayable top-up for nursing, midwifery and allied health students in England: £5,000/yr training grant + £2,000/yr parental support + travel reimbursement + exceptional support up to £3,000/yr (2026/27 confirmed). Not means-tested.
- public funds: no · NRPF: depends, requires full student finance eligibility (so refugee, HP, Ukraine, settled yes; pre-settled tuition-only and internationals no)
- get it: register with NHSBSA within 1 month of course start
- apply: https://www.nhsbsa.nhs.uk/nhs-learning-support-fund-lsf

### 122. Postgraduate loans (England)
Master's loan up to £13,206, Doctoral loan up to £31,122 (courses from 1 Aug 2026, confirmed). Same immigration categories as undergraduate finance: refugee, HP, Ukraine, stateless immediate; settled and long residence with 3 years; work/study visas excluded. Not means-tested. Note: postgraduate loans count as income for UC.
- public funds: no · NRPF: depends on category
- apply: https://www.gov.uk/masters-loan and https://www.gov.uk/doctoral-loan

### 123. Student finance grants for parents and carers
Non-repayable add-ons for full-time undergraduates with dependants (2026/27 confirmed): Childcare Grant 85% of costs up to £199.62/wk (1 child) or £342.24/wk (2+); Parents' Learning Allowance up to £2,024/yr; Adult Dependants' Grant up to £3,545/yr. Means-tested. From 2026/27 the ADG no longer covers a dependant who ordinarily lives overseas (relevant to separated migrant families).
- public funds: no · NRPF: depends, needs full student finance eligibility
- apply: inside the main student finance application

### 124. Disabled Students' Allowance
Equipment, helpers and travel top-up for disabled students, up to £27,783/yr (2025/26 confirmed rate; 2026/27 uplift unconfirmed). Not means-tested.
- public funds: no · NRPF: depends, needs a student finance category
- apply: https://www.gov.uk/disabled-students-allowance-dsa

### 125. Social Work Bursary
Capped-allocation bursary for social work degrees (2026/27): undergraduate final 2 years £4,862.50/yr (£5,262.50 London); postgraduate up to £3,762.50 + £4,052 tuition contribution + income-assessed extras. 3 years ordinary residence + SFE-style categories.
- public funds: no · NRPF: depends
- apply: https://www.nhsbsa.nhs.uk/social-work-bursary-swb

### 126. Teacher training bursaries
Tax-free bursaries for postgraduate teacher training, 2026/27: £29,000 physics, chemistry, computing, maths (scholarships £31,000), £20,000 languages and D&T, £5,000 biology and geography. Paid monthly, no separate application. 2026/27 change: offers made after 7 May 2026 need standard student-support eligibility, closing the old international exception (so refugee, HP, Ukraine, settled qualify; Skilled Worker and Graduate visa holders no longer do).
- public funds: no · NRPF: depends on student-support category
- apply: via the ITT course, https://www.gov.uk/find-postgraduate-teacher-training-courses

### 127. Advanced Learner Loan and the Access to HE write-off
Loan covering level 3 to 6 college course fees for 19+, no means test, no credit check. The killer detail: the entire outstanding Access to HE Diploma loan is written off when you complete any designated HE course afterwards, so the Access year becomes free for adults who go on to a degree (SLC factsheet, verified). Courses from 1 Jan 2027 move to the Lifelong Learning Entitlement.
- public funds: no · NRPF: depends, same categories as student finance
- apply: https://www.gov.uk/advanced-learner-loan

### 128. Funded apprenticeships: residency rules
Right to work + leave covering the full apprenticeship + normally 3 years UK residence. Exempt from the 3-year rule (eligible from grant): refugees, HP, stateless, discretionary leave, Ukraine schemes, Afghan schemes, plus family members. EUSS can count EEA residence. NRPF itself is irrelevant. Asylum seekers effectively cannot (work restrictions).
- public funds: no · NRPF: yes if status qualifies
- source: https://www.nrpfnetwork.org.uk/information-and-resources/rights-and-entitlements/employment/apprenticeships

### 129. Care to Learn
Childcare paid while a parent under 20 is at school or college (not university, not apprenticeships): up to £180 per child per week, £195 in London (2026/27 confirmed), paid direct to the provider, plus childcare-linked travel. Follows the broad 16-19 education residency rules, which include asylum-seeking young people.
- public funds: no · NRPF: depends on 16-19 funding residency, usually yes for lawfully resident young parents
- apply: https://www.gov.uk/care-to-learn

### 130. Bank accounts for international students
Mainstream 0% student overdrafts usually need ~3 years UK address history, so new internationals rarely get them. Realistic route: app bank on arrival (Monzo, Starling, Revolut with eVisa + selfie), then an international student account (HSBC, NatWest Select, Lloyds Classic, Santander basic; no 0% overdraft). University enrolment letters often replace proof of address.
- public funds: no · NRPF: yes
- source: Save the Student international banking guide

Free routes: Citizens Advice (registered, incl. EUSS help), Law Centres (lawcentres.org.uk), Praxis (London, incl. NRPF), JCWI helpline for people without status 0800 160 1004, Settled (EUSS specialists, 20+ languages), Migrant Help (asylum, 24/7, 0808 8010 503), Refugee Council Infoline 0808 196 7272, Scottish Refugee Council.
- public funds: no · NRPF: yes, these orgs exist for precarious status · region: UK
- register: https://www.gov.uk/find-an-immigration-adviser

### 177. NHS vaccination catch-up for new arrivals
Free catch-up immunisations at any age for anyone with an incomplete or unknown vaccination history (MMR, polio, MenACWY and the rest), via the GP. UKHSA tells practices to assume unvaccinated unless there is a reliable record. Register with a GP (entry 4) and ask for a catch-up review; private clinics charge £30 to £100+ per dose for the same thing.
- public funds: no · NRPF: yes, including asylum seekers · region: UK
- source: https://www.gov.uk/guidance/immunisation-migrant-health-guide

### 178. Free English conversation clubs
Drop-in conversation groups at libraries, councils, churches and charities: no enrolment, no level test, no paperwork, unlike funded ESOL (entry 15). Find via the library events page or searching "[council] ESOL conversation". Self-study: BBC Learning English and British Council LearnEnglish, free.
- public funds: no · NRPF: yes · region: UK, varies by area

### 179. Free bike events and cycle training
Big Bike Revival (Cycling UK, England): free bike checks, repairs, learn-to-ride and maintenance events each spring and summer (500,000+ participants over 11 years). Bikeability: free cycle training for children via schools. Free adult cycle training in many councils, universal across London boroughs via TfL (some lend you a bike for the session).
- public funds: no · NRPF: yes · region: England-wide, London universal training
- find: https://www.cyclinguk.org/bigbikerevival and https://tfl.gov.uk/modes/cycling/cycle-skills

### 180. Try Before You Bike
Council-backed scheme (Peddle My Wheels) delivering a refurbished bike on a low monthly subscription with payments counting toward purchase; cancel and return anytime. Mostly London boroughs.
- public funds: no · NRPF: yes · region: participating boroughs
- apply: https://trybeforeyoubike.com/

### 181. Surplus food apps
Too Good To Go: shops' unsold food as surprise bags at about a third of retail (Aldi, Asda, Greggs and thousands more). Olio: neighbours and businesses giving away surplus food and household items completely free. Just need a phone.
- public funds: no · NRPF: yes · region: UK, best in cities

### 182. TV Licence: know when you do not need one
£180/yr avoided if you never watch live TV and never use BBC iPlayer (on-demand Netflix, Disney+, YouTube clips need no licence). Free at 75+ only with Pension Credit. Students in halls need cover for their room; shared houses with a joint tenancy need one licence per address; students can reclaim unused summer months.
- public funds: no · NRPF: yes · region: UK
- check: https://www.tvlicensing.co.uk/check-if-you-need-one
